## 1. Charter

Formalize the user's question (vault note `_26228180341.md`): "Things farther away appear smaller; two-point linear perspective approximates the eye. What if the geometry is non-Archimedean/ultrametric instead — would we perceive the same thing, and what would that mean ontologically?" as a UMP paper with four moves:

1. **Archimedean anatomy of perspective** — apparent size ≈ h/d rests on similar triangles, additive distance along geodesics, and well-defined angles: all Archimedean machinery.
2. **Ultrametric demolition** — in an ultrametric space every triangle is isosceles, balls are clopen and boundaryless (every point of a ball is its center), the space is totally disconnected, and there are no nonconstant continuous paths from a real interval. Each ingredient of linear perspective fails: no continuous rays, no angles, no smooth size gradient, no unique vanishing point.
3. **Rendering lemma** — the Monna map (p-adic → real, surjective, non-continuous) is an explicit candidate for a discrete→continuous perceptual interface.
4. **Underdetermination theorem** — identical first-person appearances are compatible with both metrics; perception constrains the *interface*, not the substrate metric.

## 2. Core claim (locked at P6 — see artifacts/core-claim.md)

Phenomenology cannot decide the metric: an Archimedean substrate and an ultrametric substrate rendered through a Monna-map-like interface are perceptually indistinguishable from inside.
