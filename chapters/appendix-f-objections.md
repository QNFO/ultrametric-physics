## Appendix F: Systematic Objections and Responses

**Objection 1:** "This is just mathematics, not physics."
**Response:** Every physical theory is mathematics. The question is which mathematics correctly describes nature. The ultrametric framework makes quantitative, falsifiable predictions (CMB oscillations, $a_\mu$, $M_W$) that distinguish it from Archimedean alternatives.

**Objection 2:** "Where is the Lagrangian?"
**Response:** The tree action $S_T[\Phi]$ is the fundamental dynamical principle. In the continuum limit, it reproduces the Standard Model Lagrangian plus general relativity. The Lagrangian is an effective description, not the fundamental ontology.

**Objection 3:** "How can spacetime be discrete if Lorentz symmetry holds to such precision?"
**Response:** Lorentz symmetry emerges from tree automorphisms. Violations scale as $\delta c/c \sim q^{-d}$, suppressed by tree depth. Current bounds are consistent with this scaling.

**Objection 4:** "Why haven't we seen p-adic effects before?"
**Response:** The Monna map $M_p$ is the interface with classical measurement. All standard experiments project through $M_p$, hiding the p-adic structure. The effects appear in precision anomalies ($g-2$, $M_W$, $R_K$) precisely because these probe the projection's limitations.

**Objection 5:** "This is not testable."
**Response:** Chapters 16-18 describe 18 specific, quantitative, falsifiable experimental protocols spanning collider physics, cosmology, dark matter detection, and tabletop quantum simulation.
