## Prologue: Why Ultrametric?

Every physical theory makes a geometric commitment, whether acknowledged or not. Newtonian mechanics commits to Euclidean space. General relativity commits to Lorentzian manifolds. Quantum field theory commits to Minkowski spacetime. In all these theories, distance satisfies the **Archimedean property** and the standard triangle inequality:

$$d(A, C) \leq d(A, B) + d(B, C)$$

Yet there exists a **stronger** inequality — the **ultrametric inequality**:

$$d(A, C) \leq \max\{d(A, B),\, d(B, C)\}$$

This strengthening creates a geometry where: every triangle is isosceles; every point inside a ball is its center; balls nest or are disjoint; the space is totally disconnected; and small errors cannot accumulate. This is the geometry of **hierarchies**, **trees**, and **discrete scales**.

At the Planck scale and below, the universe is a hierarchically organized, tree-like structure captured by p-adic numbers and their adelic unification. The Standard Model, general relativity, and quantum mechanics all arise as effective descriptions of this deeper ultrametric reality.
